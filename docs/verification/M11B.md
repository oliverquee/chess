## 4. Psychological & Cognitive Interpretation

The empirical metrics from the 717-game analysis reveal three distinct, actionable cognitive patterns:

### 1. The "Collapse When Ahead" Asymmetry (Severe)
- **Observation:** Player move error rate in winning positions ($>+200\text{cp}$) is **119.8 cp**, which is **$2.6\times$ higher** than their play in balanced positions (**46.1 cp**).
- **Cognitive Diagnosis:** Premature relaxation and victory assumption. Once an advantage is achieved, tactical vigilance and defensive calculation drop sharply, allowing opponents to initiate counter-attacks or swindles.

### 2. Plan Fixation Bias (High)
- **Observation:** **581 instances** of 3+ move piece-fixation chains where the cumulative evaluation dropped $>100\text{cp}$ (**3.63 events per 100 moves**).
- **Cognitive Diagnosis:** Sunk-cost / fixation trap. When the player starts a maneuver with a specific minor piece (e.g. Knight or Bishop), they often execute the 2nd and 3rd steps of their preconceived plan mechanically, failing to re-evaluate the board state when the opponent disrupts the idea.

### 3. Tactical Passivity Dominance
- **Observation:** In positions requiring a forcing tactical move, the player plays a passive quiet move **15.9% of the time** ($2.65\times$ more frequently than over-forcing in quiet positions at **6.0%**).
- **Cognitive Diagnosis:** Reluctance to pull the trigger on complex calculations. When tactical simplifications or sacrificial checks present themselves, the player defaults to non-committal quiet moves.

### 4. Emotional Composure & Speed
- **Tilt Index:** At **1.25** (well below the $>1.4$ tilt cascade threshold), the player demonstrates resilience following large tactical blunders, rarely entering multi-move panic spirals.
- **First-Idea Bias:** At **11.6%**, the player makes sound intuitive decisions under time pressure, with speed errors clustered in concrete tactical transitions rather than general blitz play.
